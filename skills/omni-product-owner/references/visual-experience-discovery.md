# Guided visual-experience discovery

Use at Stage D for a net-new product or explicit reconception, after the verified
A–C handoff. Existing selected products, focused repairs and faithful migrations
reuse current decisions. Retain volunteered preferences earlier without starting
product design. This is a short conversation, not an engine or chart checklist.

## Ask from the evidence

Explain briefly what the analysis supports, recommend one useful direction with a
reason, and ask this first question only when its answer is missing:

> How would you like people to explore this data? You can choose several experiences, or have us recommend a mix based on the questions your team needs to answer.

Tailor the menu to the source and intended surface:

| Experience | Examples | Evidence needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Compare and track | Bars, trends, distributions, rankings | Comparable measures/populations, supported time coverage and denominators. |
| Explore locations | Maps, regional patterns, spatial relationships | Verified locations and precision, or an explicitly labeled schematic. Names alone do not establish coordinates. |
| Inspect objects or environments | Products, buildings, equipment, assemblies | State whether geometry is illustrative, dimensionally referenced or linked to governed entities. |
| Follow movement and flow | Recorded replay, routes, connected processes | Distinguish ordered identity/time/position observations from aggregate flows and illustrative explanation. |
| Create atmosphere and context | Imagery, cutaways, backgrounds, illustrations | Purpose, source/rights, placement and accessible alternative; imagery is not measured evidence. |

Users may describe other experiences. Explain an unsupported option conditionally,
with the exact data or runtime gap; do not promise every family. Keep brand, website,
logo authority, device needs and delivery scope in the existing discovery batch.
This menu supplements those decisions.

## Conditional comparison treatment

If comparisons or trends are requested **or part of the recommended scope**, and
treatment is unresolved, ask:

> For bar charts, line charts and other comparisons, would you prefer refined 2D, a futuristic dimensional appearance, interactive 3D, or a recommended mix? Dimensional options can use sculpted columns, layered trends and lighting; interactive 3D adds useful camera and inspection controls. Which fits the experience you want?

| Choice | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Refined 2D | Deliberate composition, typography, annotation and interaction without spatial depth. |
| Dimensional styling | Depth, materials and light with a stable analytical view; a 2.5D treatment need not be a navigable scene. |
| Interactive 3D | Useful inspection views, selection, camera controls and an exact-value alternative; qualify the target/runtime before committing. |
| Recommend a mix | Explicitly delegate treatment to the designer within the stated task, brand and constraints. |

Recommend by task, not by a package-wide favorite. A clear supplied answer such as
“use futuristic dimensional charts” closes this question. Ordinary material, camera
and spacing choices then belong to design, without another interview. Futuristic
does not mean mandatory neon, dark mode, glass, rotation or depth on every chart.

## Reuse and branch rules

| Situation | Next action |
| --- | --- |
| Relevant answer already supplied | Reuse its actual source and scope; do not ask again. |
| Comparisons/trends included, treatment unanswered | Ask the follow-up; hold affected new concepts while independent evidence work continues. |
| First answer is “recommend a mix” | This delegates family recommendations only unless the reply also delegates treatment. Ask the follow-up when the recommended scope includes comparisons. |
| User explicitly delegates all visual treatments | Retain that scope and source; designer proposes a supported treatment. Stage E still requires the actual product choice. |
| No comparison/trend scope | Retain a reasoned not-applicable disposition consistent with current requirements. |
| Answer absent, ambiguous or conflicting | Keep the material question unresolved. Silence and elapsed time are not delegation. |

Use conditional questions when the host supports them; otherwise ask the follow-up
after the first answer. A single-choice widget cannot represent multiple selections:
accept a short free-text list instead. Do not make the user coordinate specialists.

## Carry the decision forward

Before E, recover the experience's distinguishing qualities as well as its visual
family. If a consequential answer is missing, ask a compact question about what
the experience should feel and do differently: composition, imagery, depth,
interaction, motion or spatial inspection; qualities to borrow and avoid; and
first versus repeat use only when that changes the journey. Do not require an
answer for every dimension. Keep explicit quality delegation separate from family
or comparison-treatment delegation. An analyst persona does not imply “simple,”
and an expressive brief does not mandate imagery, 3D or a library.

Bundle unresolved choices when useful: “Which visual families support your task,
which comparison treatment appeals to you, and what should make this experience
feel distinctive? You can delegate any or all three recommendations.” Record the
actual scope of that reply in each existing index; a single clearly scoped reply
can supply all three. Do not expand “recommend the charts” into authority to choose
the quality ambition, install a library, or select the final product option.

Translate the actual answer into observable acceptance attached to existing
requirements, scoped to the affected destination or journey. Use
`product.quality_bar` from the linked canonical contract to index those rows.
Reuse sources rather than asking again, preserve unresolved answers, and keep
recommendations attributable as proposals. Tooling restrictions change observation
limits; they do not silently reduce the requested quality.

Use existing requirements and attributable private notes for family membership,
treatment, priority, constraints, source and acceptance. Separate a user statement,
reused decision, explicit delegation, agent recommendation and unresolved preference.
Use the current [product-intent contract](../../omni-artifact-accelerator/references/product-intent.md)
for its versioned discovery index and the [stage gate](../../omni-artifact-accelerator/references/stage-gates.md)
for D evidence; neither is a second ledger or independent authentication.

At E, each of exactly three feasible products must honor the current preferences,
use the same analytical narrative and show a representative comparison treatment
when relevant. Do not turn the four choices above into a fixed concept trio. Wait
for the actual product selection before detailed design/code. Reopening material
discovery goes through the existing stage replay rules; never reuse a choice for a
changed option set or quietly strip a requirement on resume.

Compare concrete representative visuals with realistic labels and a consequential
state. Each option keeps the same active quality requirements, truthful analytical
scope and relevant treatment; vary structure and primary interaction. An original
static SVG/storyboard is useful evidence when rendering is unavailable, but it
does not establish observed craft. Prose/ASCII alone is insufficient. For products
with both surfaces, each option covers both. Recommend with reasons and limitations;
the user's actual product choice remains separate from structural validation.

After selection, hand treatment decisions to the designer using
[dimensional visualization craft](../../omni-design-systems/references/dimensional-visualization-craft.md).
Return only consequential unresolved questions centrally. Responsibilities do not
prove that workers were dispatched. A visual preference grants no dependency,
publication, surface-switch or live-system authority.
